Stroke Prediction and Information


Welcome to my website! This is an interactive website designed for those who are concerned about their health, in particular with their heart and their chances of suffering from a stroke. Please note that this website is designed to give purely important and relative information about the risks of suffering from a stroke. This website also provides general recommendations based on these questions that you will answer from backed-up sources and the information provided below from our dataset of over 5000 participants who offered to give their information and experiences during their life.


Stroke is the 2nd leading cause of death globally and is responsible for approximately 11% of total deaths, according the World Health Organisation (WHO). There are many reasons why one unfortunately suffers from a stroke, however, we have been able to gather information on the key factors that contribute to one's chances of getting a stroke. These factors include: gender, age, hypertension (i.e. high blood pressure), heart disease, average glucose levels, and BMI index.


Note: All information submitted by the user via answering the questions below will be saved for research and analytical reasons for us to be able to provide you with important information. For further queries about your health please visit your GP or a specialist since this website can only provide so much and it is best to consult a professional. This website is only designed to inform you of risks of suffering from a stroke and give you general advice in order to better your health and decrease your chances of getting a stroke.

The pie chart below shows the number of participents who suffered from a stroke and whether or not they suffer from hypertension (high blood pressure).

As you can see, there is a clear correlation between whether or not the participants suffered from hypertension and whether or not they had a stroke. The number of people who suffer from hypertension and had a stroke is much higher than those who do not suffer from hypertension and had a stroke.



The pie chart below shows the number of participents who suffered from a stroke and whether or not they suffer from any sort of heart disease.

The same goes for heart disease. There is a clear correlation between whether or not the participants suffered from any heart disease and whether or not they had a stroke. The number of people who suffer from heart disease and have had a stroke is much higher compared to those who do not suffer from heart disease and have had a stroke.


The scatter plot below shows the correlation between BMI index and Average Glucose Levels of patients who suffered from a stroke.

There is little connection between the BMI index and the Average Glucose Level of a patient who suffered from a stroke, however, there is a slight positive slope in the trend line between these two variables.
This indicates that there is some connection between the two, and the graph shows how high BMI index and high Average Glucose Levels are a common trend between those who suffered from a stroke.


The histogram below shows the age of participants who had suffered from a stroke.
There can be seen a slight increase in stroke cases among those who are older, suggesting that stroke cases increase with the increase in age.


The bar chart below shows the smoking status of the participants and how many people did and did not suffer from a stroke in each category.



General observations:

  • In the 'Unknown' category, out of 1483 people in total, 29 people suffered from a stroke (~2%).

  • In the 'formerly smoked' category, out of 836 people in total, 57 people suffered from a stroke (~7%).

  • In the 'never smoked' category, out of 1852 people in total, 84 people suffered from a stroke (~4.5%).

  • In the 'smokes' category, out of 737 people in total, 39 people suffered from a stroke (~5.3%).

In general, there is not much of a difference in percentages between the four categories, however, the graph does suggest that those who have formerly smoked and those who do smoke are at a higher risk of suffering from a stroke.
